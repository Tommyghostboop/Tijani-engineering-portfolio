01 / Profile

Engineering with
hands-on depth.

Maintenance is a discipline, not a reaction. Most of what I do happens before anything breaks.

I trained as a mechanical engineer at the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta, and I've spent the time since on workshop floors rather than away from them — heavy plant, engines, and the maintenance systems that decide whether either keeps running.

At IITA I started as a heavy-equipment maintenance intern and was retained as contract staff. The work is preventive schedules, predictive routines and corrective repair — plus the part most people skip: writing down what was found, what was done and what it means for next time. A maintenance record nobody can read is the same as no record at all.

When maintenance alone can't solve the problem, I build. A tyre debeading machine that was punishing the person operating it. A sensor rig that calls an overflow before it happens. The instinct is the same either way: find the failure mode, then remove it.

03 / Selected Work

Projects built
to solve problems.

Each one started as something that wasn't working — a slow process, a hidden failure, a load path nobody could see.

MACHINE DESIGN / BUILD

Tyre Debeading Machine

Separating a tyre bead by hand is slow, and it is hard on the person doing it. I designed, fabricated and then modified a powered debeading machine — reworking the frame for rigidity under load and repositioning the controls so the operator isn't fighting the machine to use it. Faster bead separation, less downtime, and a job that stops being punishing.

DESIGN / CAD

Tyre Debeader — CAD Model

The machine modelled before it was cut: frame geometry, work surface, the bead-separation head and the belt-driven motor slung underneath. Settling the clearances and the drive layout on screen meant settling them once — not twice in steel.

AUTOMOTIVE / OVERHAUL

Engine Disassembly & Overhaul

Stripped a four-cylinder engine to component level to map what every part does and how it fails, then worked overhauls on both four-cylinder petrol and six-cylinder diesel units. Valve train, piston assemblies, bearing shells, torque sequence — the reason a component fails is usually written on its wear face, if you know how to read it.

Engine SystemsOverhaulDiagnosticsTolerances
AUTOMATION / SENSING

Water Level Detection & Alarm

An overflow is a failure you can see coming. Working with junior engineers, I prototyped an ultrasonic sensor that measures the distance to the water surface and fires a buzzer once that distance crosses the set point. It is the same loop as condition monitoring — measure, compare to a threshold, act early — applied to a tank instead of a bearing.

Ultrasonic SensingThreshold LogicPrototyping
STRUCTURES

Eiffel Tower Structural Prototype

A scaled space-frame model built from low-cost material to make load paths visible. Every member is either pushing or pulling, and triangulation is what stops the geometry folding under its own eccentricity — easy to state on paper, and much harder to forget once you have felt a badly braced bay go soft in your hands.

Space FrameTriangulationLoad PathsPrototyping
ELECTRICAL / FABRICATION

Portable Low-Power LED Lighting

A battery-powered lighting unit designed around runtime rather than peak brightness — current-limited parallel strings, sized so the pack lasts a full working session instead of an impressive first hour. Wiring the strings in parallel was deliberate: a single open string dims the unit, it doesn't switch it off.
